Historically, Telugus used decimal system for writing integers, but used base 4 system for writing the fractions part.

This simple app helps you convert decimal part to telugu base 4 notation and the equivalent telugu descriptors.
For more, read [this](https://blog.plover.com/math/telugu.html) fascinating blog post.

## How It Works

The decimal part is broken down by base4 representation, so they all add up.

Based on whether a digit occurs in even or odd positions, they use different markings. Here are the different markings:

| Digit | Odd Position | Even Position |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| 0     | ౸            | ౦             |
| 1     | ౹            | ౼             |
| 2     | ౺            | ౽             |
| 3     | ౻            | ౾             |

With this system, you can encode fractional parts of numbers in a unique Telugu base 4 notation that has been used historically for measuring volumes and quantities.

This demonstrates the mathematical sophistication of traditional Telugu culture and their practical approach to representing fractional quantities in everyday commerce and measurement.
