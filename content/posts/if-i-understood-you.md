# If I Understood You, Would I Have This Look on My Face?

By Alan Alda

I adore Alan Alda in Woody Allen movies like *Crimes and Misdemeanors* or *Everyone Says I Love You*. But there's a lot more to him than acting. Beyond movies, Alda has spent years working at the intersection of science and communication. He hosted PBS's *Scientific American Frontiers* for over a decade, founded the Alan Alda Center for Communicating Science at Stony Brook University, and has written books on how we can relate to each other more effectively.

This book isn't a memoir like his earlier ones. Instead, it's a compact, story-driven guide to the art and science of connecting with other people.

Alda argues that communication is no soft skill—it can be the difference between life and death.

- If patients don't understand their doctors, they ignore treatments.
- If engineers can't convince a town a dam is about to fail, disaster strikes.
- If a parent can't reach their child about drugs, tragedy may follow.

What makes the book work is Alda's trademark storytelling. He takes you everywhere—from a dentist's chair (in a disastrous appointment that became his personal symbol of poor communication), to his early days knocking on doors selling mutual funds, to his run-ins with scientists he occasionally annoyed with clumsy questions. He never hides his mistakes; instead, he frames them as lessons.

Alda combines improv theater, acting techniques, and modern science into a toolkit for better communication:

- Empathy: The fundamental ingredient—feeling *with* another person.
- Theory of Mind: Our detective skill for guessing what others are thinking or feeling by reading tone, body language, or word choice.
- Improv Exercises: Try delivering bad news in gibberish to practice body language, or mirror a partner's movements to feel "in sync."
- Storytelling: Whether it's a scientist needing funding, a business pitching an idea, or a parent warning a child, Alda insists a good, vivid story sticks better than jargon.
- Unlearning: So much of communication means undoing habits—technical language, canned scripts, or stiff body presence—that separate us instead of connecting us.

At under 200 pages, this isn't a manual—it's an invitation to look harder, listen deeper, and treat every conversation as a chance to connect. Alda writes the way he acts: with warmth, wit, and just enough self-mockery to keep you leaning in.
