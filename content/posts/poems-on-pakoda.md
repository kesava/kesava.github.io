# Poems on Pakoda

**By Chilakamarthi Lakshmi Narasimham**
*(From early 20th century; translated from Telugu)*

The humble pakoda—that crispy, golden fritter beloved across India—inspired this Telugu poet to compose verses of mythological proportions. Here's a delightful celebration of everyone's favorite monsoon snack.

---

*vanitala palukula yanduna
nanimiṣa lōkamuna nunna damr̥tamaṭuncun
janulanu ṭegāni lēdaṭa
kanugo na nī yandamr̥tamu galadu pakōḍī!*

They say among humans,
nectar lives in a woman's words.
But the truest nectar I have found
is in you, Pakoda.

---

*ā kammadanamu nā ruci
yā karakara yā ghamaghumamayāpomakamulā
rākalu pōkalu vaḍupulu
nīkēdagu nendulēvu nijamu pakōḍī॥*

That deliciousness, that rich taste,
that crunch, that soul-stirring aroma,
the crackle, the dance around the mouth
none wear it better than you, Pakoda.

---

*nī karakara nādaṁbulu
mā karṇāmr̥tamulu nīdu mahitākr̥tiyē
mā kanula candamāmaga
nēkoni yāḍe danu summa ninnu pakōḍī॥*

Your crunch and snap
are music to my ears.
You shine like the moon to me
I will sing your praises, Pakoda.

---

*ā rāmānujuḍā gati
pōruna mūrchilla decce manu sanjīvin
māruti yerugaḍu gāka ya
yyāreninugoni na bratukaḍaṭa pakōḍī॥*

When Rama's brother fell faint,
Hanuma raced to find the Sanjeevi herb.
Had he known better,
he would have run for you, Pakoda.

---

*kōḍini dinuṭaku selavun
vēḍiri munnubrāhmaṇulu vēdha nantaḍun
kōḍi valadā badulu pa
kōḍindinumanucu jeppe kūrmi pakōḍī॥*

When the Brahmins sought leave to eat chicken,
Brahma declared, "No chicken for you!"
"Instead," said the creator with a smile,
"Feast on Pakodi — love your Pakoda."

---

## A Note on the Translation

These verses showcase the Telugu literary tradition's ability to blend high and low culture—elevating a street snack to the realm of mythology with references to Ramayana and Puranic stories. The playful use of *pakōḍī* (pakoda) as both the subject of devotion and a pun in the final verse (playing on *kōḍi*, chicken) demonstrates the wit and wordplay that characterize Telugu poetry.

The crunch of a fresh pakoda becomes as divine as nectar, and its crispy texture is worthy of more praise than the legendary Sanjeevi herb. This is food poetry at its finest—reverent, ridiculous, and absolutely delicious.
