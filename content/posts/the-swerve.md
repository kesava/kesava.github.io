# The Swerve

**By Stephen Greenblatt**

The book tells the story of how a forgotten ancient poem helped shape the modern world.

In the 15th century, a book hunter named Poggio Bracciolini stumbled upon a long poem "On the Nature of Things" by the Roman poet Lucretius, in a monastery.

## The Radical Poem

The poem, written centuries earlier, described a universe made of tiny "atoms" (the word comes from here), dismissed the idea of gods controlling everything, and encouraged people to seek happiness without fear.

The author argues that rediscovering this book helped spark big changes in the Renaissance and beyond, influencing science, free thought, and the way we see the world today.

## The World of Book Hunting

The book spends considerable amount of time on Poggio's book hunting. And in that process, it explores various scribes, book binding technologies, role of monasteries in preserving knowledge, fonts, palimpsests, different kinds of "paper" used by scribes etc. It's all fascinating. I ended up googling and learning a bunch about Indian scribes and book binding technologies too.

## Radical Then, Radical Now

Even before Christianity, Lucretius' ideas were seen as radical. Romans admired Stoicism and duty, while Epicureanism (which promoted simple pleasures and the absence of fear) was often misunderstood as hedonistic. Christianity encouraged people to strive for pain instead. (Hinduism seems like a mix of stoicism and epicureanism here, it encourages to strive for dharma-artha-kama. Moksha is a later addition, apparently)

## The Writing

The author has a fantastic lightness and beauty to his writing. He talks about deeply serious topics with such breezy agility, it's a page turner. No wonder it won a Pulitzer for nonfiction.

This is a story about how ideas never truly die—they hide in monasteries, wait in dusty libraries, and emerge centuries later to change everything again.
